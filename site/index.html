Christchurch Underground

What's under the ground in Christchurch, starting with four lines of cone penetration tests (CPTs) from Oregon State University and 5,946 water wells and bores from Environment Canterbury. Three of the CPT sites liquefied in the 2010–11 earthquakes; one didn't. Pick a site to see where it is, then scroll down to see the ground beneath it, or explore the sites in 3D.

Large orange dots are OSU CPTs; hollow dots are NZGD investigations on the same lines, located by OSU. Small dots are ECan wells (2,988 of them have a logged soil profile), coloured by depth. Use the layer button (top right) to show or hide them; click any dot for details.

Each column is one CPT, coloured by Robertson (2010) soil behaviour type. That's inferred from how the soil resists the cone, not from samples, and uses the non-normalised index (no groundwater or unit-weight correction). CPTs at each site are 1.5 m apart, so differences between neighbours show how much the ground varies over very short distances. Dashed outlines are NZGD investigations on the same line, which aren't available here.
